## Charging

### Battery Health best practices (as per 2026)
- must charge occasionally to 100%, for calibration and cell balancing. Car head unit says once a week `¯\_₍ッ₎_/¯`
- do not leave car parked at 100% battery SOC for "long" (weeks?)
- sweet spot seems to be between 30 and 70 percent for "most of the time".
- don't fast charge *all the time*
- prefer deeper discharge, with less frequent charge sessions that then charge more (80% or 100%)
- don't worry to much about it, LFP chemistry is fairly forgiving (as compared to other chemistries).

Information on best practices vary in detail from month-to-month it seems, but the core ideas so far seems stable.

[Here is a reasonable resource at time for writing](https://unanswered.io/guide/optimal-charging-lfp-batteries-100-percent)

The charger can be installed by any qualified electrician. Be sure to obtain a Certificate of Compliance (CoC) as well.

When charging at max AC capacity (32 Amps on the Energy app on your car's head unit), the wallbox will pull +-30A from your power (7kW/h). 
This is considered to be a pretty large load for a residential circuit, but should be completely safe. BUT, be aware, that you might want to ensure other heavy appliances (geyser, stove etc) are not active while charging at full capacity.

### In Public
For public **AC** charging, you will often require your own Type 2 AC charge cable. There are local persons and operations that can supply this.

For **DC** charging, cable will be permanently attached to the charger.

My advice is always,if you are a first time EV owner, make a deliberate test run to a public charger, when you are calm and have plenty of time, to go and try the public charging. It is not hard, but often you need a few minutes to get the apps working. RFID cards makes this a lot easier, which is why that is advised as "must" have above.

Not all **C**harge **P**oint **O**perators use RFID cards though, so you might still need apps. 
You will need the apps even with RFID cards, since that is the most convenient way to top-up your account.

Some CPOs are rolling out credit card payments. This might make things easier - time will tell.

### Portable charger
The portable charger does max 16 Amp, or +- 3kW/h.

South African household plugs are generally rated for **max** 16 Amps. 
Unless you are **very** sure about your home plug wiring, it is advised to limit your portable charging session to a lower Amp setting via the car's built in "Energy" app on the head unit.
10A is generally considered on the safe side by the community.

The problem is that even if your plug can handle 16A for a short while (5min?), your car will be charging for *hours*. We have reports of lower quality wall plugs melting at 16A charging. No body likes Surprise Fire - better safe than sorry!

#### Extension cables
**DANGER - the following is advice you use at your OWN risk**

The official, sensible, guidance is "do NOT use an extension cable with your portable charger".

However, the official portable charger has insufficient reach (cable length) for many situation. 
For those case, you can consider a properly specced extension cable, which will be of at least 10AWG (or +- 2.5mm^2),copper, rated at 16A, no less.
Janus coupler and South African plug also rated for 16A, of high quality.

Test this for at least an hour to ensure not part of the connection is heating up.

Ideally, limit your car charging to 10A (Energy app on the head unit).

Remember, NOBODY likes Surprise Fire. 

## Discharging (V2L)
The E2 supports discharging (i.e. supplying power to external loads) via it's charge port, up to +-3kW/h

You will need to obtain a suitable V2L cable/adapter for this function however, since, like so many other things, it is not supplied with the sale of the vehicle.

Please ensure you obtain *high* quality equipment, since this it not an area you want to invite Surprise Fire.

### Connecting V2L with your home
An interesting option, assuming suitable V2L equipment, is to enable your car to feed power into your home inverter or DB possibly.

That means you can directly charge your PV system battery from your car, or feed some house hold loads, with the car.

Note, again, qualified electrician required.
